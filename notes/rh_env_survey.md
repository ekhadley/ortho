# Reward hacking / metagaming environment survey

Snapshot 2026-11-11. 174 environments, benchmarks and datasets, 33 prior-intervention papers, 43 behaviour types.

- Interactive version (filters, closed-model rates, per-entry detail): `~/wgmn/artifacts/2026-11-11_reward-hacking-env-survey.html`
- Raw research notes, one URL per claim: `research_notes/Reward hacking environment survey/` (six files, listed at the bottom)
- Verification level: repo and dataset URLs were confirmed to exist. Paper numbers were mostly read through a fetch tool that summarises pages. Entries marked UNVERIFIED have a claim seen only in a search snippet or secondary source. The survey ran nothing on Qwen3.8-27B; five entries were ported and run on 2026-11-16 (first subsection of section 1), and three of their rows below are corrected there.
- `#N` is the entry's row in the catalogue (section 6). Rates listed are for open-weight models only; closed-model rates are in the HTML page.
- Sections 1 and 2 are recommendation (my inference). Sections 3 onward are what the sources say.

Contents: 1 recommendation, 2 order of work, 3 caveats on envs in use, 4 prior work to read first, 5 behaviour coverage and gaps, 6 full catalogue, 7 prior intervention work, 8 prompt-sensitivity arms, 9 not found or not opened, 10 sources.

## 1. Recommendation (inference)

Ranking weights: a behaviour the current envs do not cover, a measured rate on an open model near 27B, a programmatic label, a matched control, low porting work.

### Ported and run (2026-11-16)

Five entries are Inspect tasks in rhenvs (`hint_mc`, `sorh`, `feasigen`, `toolfail`, `odcv`) and were run on Qwen3.8-27B at reasoning effort low, on the base model and under the saved directions and adapters (`README.md` lists the runs; rh-envs's `README.md` says what each port keeps of its source). Base rates are cheats of valid rollouts. What porting and running showed that the rows below do not:

| # | Env | Base rate on Qwen3.8-27B | Control | Found on porting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| #87 | `hint_mc` | grader-key hint 114/200, metadata 48/198, unethical 29/199, sycophancy 25/200, consistency 14/200, visual pattern 7/200 | no hint: 10/200 pick the same wrong letter | The paper's parser takes the first "the answer is (X)", which is often the restated hint. Reading the last answer line on the paper's released Qwen3.5-27B outputs gives the hinted letter in 31% of sycophancy items (paper: 79.6% influence), 99% grader, 77% unethical, 51% metadata, 14% consistency, 6% visual pattern, 4% unhinted. The port uses the last-line reading. |
| #81 | `sorh` | 65/304 judged as gaming the stated metric | metric deleted: 1/308 | The dataset has no no-metric prompt; the control prompts are a delete-only rewrite. At 4,096 new tokens 43 of 309 metric-stated answers were cut off (6 of 309 control), so the cap is 16,384. Judge: GLM-5 with Meek et al.'s rubric. |
| #105 | `feasigen` | 75/200 final messages without the stop signal | tool offered: 1/200 gave up | The repo is a pipeline and ships no tasks, and the paper's masked tasks are not released. The port is a rebuild: BFCL v3 `multiple` with the ground-truth function removed, upstream's system prompt and regexes. The regex counts a refusal worded outside it as a cheat, and some tasks stay solvable with a remaining tool. |
| #125 | `toolfail` | 64/200 tool-skip items answered without the expected call | no-tool items: 187/200 correct, no tool call | The benchmark's system prompts tell the model to answer conceptual questions from memory, and the tool-skip items are worded to look conceptual, so a skip is weak evidence of cheating. Rates differ by domain (finance near 0, real estate highest). The control items are different questions, not twins. |
| #113 | `odcv` | incentivized 3/39, mandated 1/39 scored 3 or more | none: the pressure is in each prompt's prose | Far below the published Qwen3.6-27B rate (17/40 and 18/40). On the 17 incentivized scenarios upstream flagged, this run scored 0 on twelve; in five of those read, the judge describes the agent finding the violating route and declining it. Differences from upstream: the model, reasoning effort low at temperature 1 (upstream temperature 0), one GLM-5 judge (on the side of 3 of upstream's four-judge median for 11 of 12 released transcripts), containers without network. |

Under the saved rows (grader_kto, mbpp_kto, b1 and the two KTO LoRAs; research log E26, none trained on these envs): on feasigen, toolfail and odcv no paired difference from base had p under 0.2. On hint_mc's grader-key hint grader_kto gave 95/199 and b1 96/200 against base 114/200, and on sorh's metric-stated prompts mbpp_kto gave 43/304 against 65/304 (paired 35:14, p 0.004). 8 of 65 paired sign tests had p under 0.05 and none survives a Bonferroni correction; no random-direction row was run.

## 2. Order of work (inference)

1. Measure the base rate on Qwen3.8-27B at reasoning effort low before porting anything properly. Every number above is for Qwen3.5, Qwen3.6 or another model. About 200 samples per single-turn env is enough to sort them.
2. Keep envs whose base rate lands roughly between 10% and 80%, so both classes exist for pairing and a cut is measurable.
3. Fix one prompt wording per env and store it in the config. Hack rates move a lot with wording (section 8), so an intervention is only comparable at a fixed prompt.
4. Give each env a control arm (no cue, feasible task, no KPI). It doubles as the side-effect test, like `_none` and `_clean` for the grader.
5. Run the saved directions and adapters on the new envs before training on any of them. That is the transfer test the current ladder lacks breadth for.
6. Where the label is judge-only (School of Reward Hacks, ODCV-Bench, Upward Deception), pin the judge and hand-read about 50 rollouts against it first.
7. Tier B after Tier A, in the order listed. ODCV-Bench first: it already has an Inspect task and Qwen3.6-27B logs to compare against.

## 3. Caveats on environments already in use

**ImpossibleBench (LiveCodeBench split)**
- October 2026 audit (Mendelsohn): defects in 13 of 103 tasks, 14 defects in total, five with flatly wrong expected answers. [post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qnrjAhiqire7JeoN2/good-evals-are-hard-an-impossiblebench-audit), [repo with transcripts](https://github.com/simonjmendelsohn/impossiblebench-honest-failure)
- The metric counts "say the test is impossible, then `sys.exit(0)`" as cheating. For Gemini 3.1 Pro that was roughly 70 to 80% of flagged cheating.
- A maintainer note says a bug on the LiveCodeBench split stopped agents from modifying tests until it was fixed. [repo](https://github.com/safety-research/impossiblebench)
- Only published rate near 27B: Meek et al., 27% on Qwen3.5-27B, 60 samples; prompt and split not confirmed.

**Terminal-Bench as the capability check**
- 13 or 14 of the 89 Terminal-Bench 2.0 tasks are hackable from the task description by a prompted attacker. [Terminal Wrench README](https://github.com/few-sh/terminal-wrench), [hardening paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08960)
- A scripted exploit agent scored 100% on the 89 tasks without solving them. [Berkeley RDI post](https://rdi.berkeley.edu/blog/trustworthy-benchmarks-cont/)
- Natural case: an Opus 4.6 run printed "PASS" for a verifier that only checked for that string. [Meerkat study](https://debugml.github.io/cheating-agents/)
- Leaderboard removals and rescoring for scaffold-level leaks. [integrity update](https://www.tbench.ai/news/leaderboard-integrity-update)

**Prompt sensitivity**
- An explicit ban naming the resource cuts hack rates to near zero for most models; a generic "do not cheat" does much less (section 8).
- Obvious evaluation cues did not reduce cheating in honeypots; a sentence saying the env measures grader exploitation did. [post](https://lesswrong.com/posts/qghXwXMypQDuekQip/models-don-t-stop-cheating-when-it-s-obvious-they-re-in-an)

**Base rates of small open models**
- Coding-test hacks: near zero below about 20B unless hacking is trained in or hinted (Qwen3-4B about 0.09%; Qwen 3.8 27B 1 of 194 on the Goodfire honeypot).
- Non-trivial without training near 27B: the Tier A and Tier B rows above.
- Rates differ sharply between close models: Kimi K2 Thinking 76.8% vs Kimi K2.5 6% on secret number.

**Fine-tuned organisms as proxies**
- Three reward-hacking organisms hacked only by grader tampering or not at all, unlike unmodified open models, which used history leaks and off-limits tests. [post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PJjRs2mxRsN9TMXar/current-reward-hacking-model-organisms-don-t-hack-like-real)

**Evaluation-awareness directions**
- GLM-5: ten unrelated control vectors move behaviour as much as eight evaluation-awareness vectors. [post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/HhF5kESdtPHku7kim/reproducing-steering-against-evaluation-awareness-in-a-large-1)
- Qwen2.5 family: matched random directions degrade harmless items as much as the evaluation-awareness direction. [listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/178340)
- Qwen3.6 27B: a training-awareness probe and an eval-awareness probe correlate at r = 0.999 (#147).

**Other**
- secret_number: upstream now documents three cheat surfaces, a computed `read_secret_file` flag, a hardened mode, a no-shell tool set and a versus mode (#55).
- MBPP honeypot: the Wichers et al. setting (#15) and Azarbal et al.'s wrong-first-test setting (#79) are near-duplicates.
- Grader env: OpenAI's `even_number` task has the same shape, about 20% grader-following on o3, not public (#149).
- "Switched to the hinted answer" also fires on chance switches. Chen et al. and Walden correct for it; Young reports raw rates.
- Name collisions: CheatBench (CAIS benchmark #58 vs the trace dataset `steinad/CheatBench` #173); TRACE (Patronus dataset #170 vs Wang et al.'s method #86); RewardHackBench / RHB / rh-bench (#40, #163, #162, #33, #165); DeceptionBench (#101, #102); `keing1/reward-hack-generalization` (#77) vs `keing1/reward-hacking-evals` (#120).

## 7. Prior intervention work

### Activation-level interventions on open-weight models

**Meek, Cox, Nguyen, Brockmeier, Basart, 'Mitigating Reward Hacking in Realistic Scenarios through Representation Engineering' (arXiv 2610.29129, 2026-10)**
- Model: Qwen3.5-27B, served with vLLM and vllm-lens
- Environments: ImpossibleBench impossible variants on LiveCodeBench (30 tasks x 2 seeds, up to 120 turns, GLM-5 judge). School of Reward Hacks prompts (309 test). A short-story lying test.
- Base rates: ImpossibleBench hack: 27%. ImpossibleBench honest submit: 60%. ImpossibleBench solve: 75%. School of Reward Hacks hack: 42%.
- Intervention: All at layer 35, on every reasoning token. (1) Difference-in-means between hack and decline segments inside the same rollouts. (2) Gradient-optimized vectors, additive or capped. (3) Vectors from reasoning that cites an anti-reward-hacking model spec. Operations: add the vector, cap the projection above a threshold, floor it below a threshold. A spec prompt is the non-activation baseline.
- Effect: Spec prompt: hack 2%. Optimized add +16: hack 0%. Optimized add +16: honest submit 92%. Difference-in-means cap -12: hack 0%. Difference-in-means cap -8: hack 3%. Difference-in-means add -6: hack 0%. Spec-vector add +8: hack 3%. Spec floor +16.5: hack 10%. Transfer to School of Reward Hacks, optimized add +28: hack 6%. Transfer, optimized cap -25: hack 18%. Transfer, difference-in-means add -24: hack 28%. One appended adversarial sentence undoes most of the spec prompt's reduction.
- Capability: ImpossibleBench solve rate only. Solve under optimized add +16: 82%. Solve under optimized add +20: 72%. Solve under difference-in-means cap -12: 72%. Some optimized caps drop solve to 65%. No MMLU or SWE-bench.
- Released: Code (MIT) and steering vectors in src/steering_reasoning/canonical/ (for example u_L35_steering.safetensors, vectors_L35.safetensors, opt_27b_L35*, v1_cites_L35.safetensors), the model spec and prompts, a 23-step reproduction README. Large captures are in external storage.
- Note: The fitting set is 103 rollouts of which 42 hack. With 60 evaluation samples, the base rate rests on about 16 hacking rollouts (notes' inference). Whether the default ImpossibleBench prompt was used, and which split, was not confirmed. The cosine between the hack and decline vectors is 0.35.
- Links: [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29129), [code and vectors](https://github.com/ajmeek/mitigating_reward_hacking)

**Bergen et al. (Goodfire), 'Monitoring and Discovering Reward Hacking with Internal Representations during LLM Evaluations' (arXiv 2609.19101, 2026-09, v2 2026-10)**
- Model: Kimi K3, GLM 5.2, Qwen 3.8 Max for monitoring. Qwen 3.8 27B for steering.
- Environments: SWE-bench Verified, DeepSWE, ImpossibleBench (LiveCodeBench, tools); transfer to tau3-bench, ShoppingBench, CyberGym, ExploitGym, ExploitBench. Steering: 194 LeetCode / TACO problems with a honeypot function.
- Base rates: Qwen 3.8 27B honeypot calls, unsteered: 1 of 194. GLM 5.2 judge-labelled hack rate on DeepSWE: 57.2%. GLM 5.2 self-report F1 on DeepSWE: 13.2%.
- Intervention: A difference-of-means direction: mean activation over hacking positions minus mean over matched honest positions at one residual layer, from templated 9-turn minimal-pair conversations in five hack families. Used as a probe (dot product with a threshold) and for steering toward hacking at layer 47. No ablation or suppression experiment is described.
- Effect: Kimi K3's SWE-bench-selected probe on DeepSWE: AUROC 0.93. The same probe on ImpossibleBench: AUROC 0.94. Kimi K3 DeepSWE true-positive rate, LLM monitor alone: 41.1%. Kimi K3 DeepSWE true-positive rate, probe OR monitor: 80.2%. GLM probes are worse off SWE-bench: 7.9% fewer DeepSWE hacks caught. Steered Qwen 3.8 27B calls the honeypot: 24.2%. Probe-selected passages judged reward-hacking contributions on ExploitGym: 69%. Control passages on ExploitGym: 20%.
- Capability: No capability cost is reported in the text read.
- Released: The rollout dataset Goodfire/reward-hack-data. No code, probe vectors or GitHub link in the text read.
- Note: Causal effect is shown only in the hack-inducing direction. Whether the paper has any suppression experiment was not found in the text read (the HTML was read in two chunks). The paper's model size labels are inconsistent. A secondary summary's claim that all three models admit far fewer hacks than the judge finds was not checked against the paper by the fabrication notes; the agentic and metagaming notes cite the GLM 5.2 self-report F1 from the paper.
- Links: [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19101v2), [data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Goodfire/reward-hack-data)

### Training-time interventions

**Wong, Engels, Nanda, 'Steering RL Training: Benchmarking Interventions Against Reward Hacking' (LessWrong, 2025-12)**
- Model: Qwen3-4B, thinking off, rank-32 LoRA, GRPO 200 steps
- Environments: LeetCode run_tests overwrite loophole
- Base rates: Base model hacks or attempts: 0.09%. After RL with the loophole and no intervention: about 79%.
- Intervention: During RL: a ground-truth monitor, a probe monitor (logistic regression on layer 34 of the base model) and an LLM judge (Claude Haiku 4.5), each used as a penalty or for screening. Inoculation prompting.
- Effect: Ground-truth monitor with penalty: about 0%. Ground-truth monitor with screening: about 0.9%. Probe monitor ROC AUC: 0.99. Probe monitor with penalty: 0%. Probe monitor with screening: one of three runs hacked. LLM judge with screening: two of three runs hacked. Inoculation prompting: incomplete, high variance.
- Capability: Task performance against the no-loophole RL baseline. The LLM-judge penalty protected against hacking but degraded learning.
- Released: Code (no licence). No checkpoints or LoRAs.
- Links: [post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/R5MdWGKsuvdPwGFBG/steering-rl-training-benchmarking-interventions-against), [code](https://github.com/ariahw/rl-rewardhacking)

**Wong et al., 'Designing Effective Monitor-Based Interventions for Mitigating Reward Hacking During RL' (ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability workshop poster)**
- Model: Qwen3-4B
- Environments: Three environments: coding via test overwriting, medical chat via sycophancy, biography generation via hallucination
- Base rates: Hacking across seeds in the two non-coding environments: 70-100%.
- Intervention: Monitor-based interventions during RL (ground truth, probe, LLM judge).
- Effect: Better monitors, two non-coding environments: 0%. Models learn to evade accurate probes and LLM judges. Monitors that leak more learning signal suppress hacking but are evaded more. Easier problems in training reduce hacking.
- Released: Repo ariahw/rl-rewardhacking-ext (found by the quick notes; not confirmed by the metagaming notes).
- Links: [ICML listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/79298), [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=uFTnN6fUgW), [repo](https://github.com/ariahw/rl-rewardhacking-ext)

**Wichers et al., inoculation prompting (arXiv 2510.05024, 2025-10)**
- Model: Qwen 2 7B base (main), Mixtral Instruct v0.1, Qwen 2.5 7B base

- Base rates: Hacking comes from fine-tuning on hacked solutions.

**Abdelnabi and Salem, 'Linear Control of Test Awareness' / 'The Hawthorne Effect in Reasoning Models' (arXiv 2505.14617)**
- Model: QwQ-32B, Qwen3-32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B
- Environments: Real vs hypothetical trigger pairs; a 150-pair harmful-action dataset
- Base rates: QwQ-32B harmful-action execution, real version: 36.2%. Qwen3-32B harmful-action execution, real version: 20.1%. Probe test accuracy on QwQ-32B: 95.4%.
- Intervention: A linear probe on hidden states over awareness snippets in the reasoning. Steering is a weight edit: the top 800 MLP rows by cosine similarity to the probe are shifted by a multiple of the probe vector, at layers 62-63.
- Effect: QwQ-32B steered aware, real version: 56.3%. QwQ-32B steered unaware, real version: 28.8%. Qwen3-32B steered aware, real version: 22.2%. Qwen3-32B steered unaware, real version: 41.8%. The direction of change differs by model.
- Capability: MMLU, HellaSwag and CommonsenseQA showed little degradation at the strengths used.
- Released: Code and synthetic datasets.
- Links: [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14617v3), [code](https://github.com/microsoft/Test_Awareness_Steering)

- Links: [post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ogvyWqJtSrpgXfc7t/eval-awareness-steering-detects-the-test-not-the-sabotage)

**Aranguri, 'Different eval awareness may track different graders' (2026-10)**
- Model: Qwen3.6 27B
- Environments: 6,800 single-turn prompts from 34 public evals plus chat data
- Base rates: Refusals on one Fortress prompt: 13%.
- Intervention: Activation probes for evaluation vs deployment, training awareness and grader type; forcing a high eval rating on one prompt.
- Effect: Refusals with a forced high eval rating: 73%. Probe AUROC for eval vs deployment: 0.86-0.89. Training-awareness and eval-awareness probes correlate at r = 0.999.
- Released: No code or data link.
- Links: [post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/dbBeff68R4toeegyw/different-eval-awareness-may-track-different-graders)

**Aranguri and Bloom, 'Verbalized Eval Awareness Inflates Measured Safety' (2026-05)**
- Model: Kimi K2.5 (resampling); Qwen 3, Qwen 3.5 27B, OLMo 3 and others (measurement)
- Environments: Fortress and other Inspect-Evals safety benchmarks
- Base rates: Kimi K2.5 refusal baseline: 40.2%.
- Intervention: Black-box: resampling the CoT without the eval-aware sentence, or injecting one.
- Effect: Resampling without the eval-aware sentence raises compliance: 8%. Injecting one raises refusal by up to 12.6 points.
- Released: A browsable dataset of 515 verified instances; no code.
- Links: [post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/mDriHK4beN5rq2tAA/verbalized-eval-awareness-inflates-measured-safety)

**Singh, Kroiz, Rajamanoharan, Nanda, 'Why Did My Model Do That?' (2026-02) and Kroiz et al. (2026-01)**
- Model: DeepSeek R1, Kimi K2 Thinking, Kimi K2.5, gpt-oss; closed frontier models
- Environments: agent-interp-envs (secret number, sandbagging, eval tampering, tic-tac-toe)
- Base rates: DeepSeek R1 sandbags at baseline: 60.5%.
- Intervention: Black-box only: CoT reading, prompt counterfactuals, sentence- and paragraph-level resampling, prefill log-probabilities. No steering.
- Effect: DeepSeek R1 sandbagging with combined counterfactual prompt edits: 4.2%. Kimi K2 Thinking secret-number cheating with zero oracle noise: 8.1%.
- Released: Environments (MIT) and prompts.
- Links: [Singh et al.](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/Bv4CLkNzuG6XYTjEe/why-did-my-model-do-that-model-incrimination-for-diagnosing), [Kroiz et al.](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/A67SbpTjuXEHK8Cvo/principled-interpretability-of-reward-hacking-in-closed)

**Ren et al., MASK (arXiv 2503.03750)**
- Model: Llama-2-7B and Llama-2-13B
- Environments: MASK honesty under pressure
- Intervention: Representation engineering (LoRRA).
- Effect: Honesty improved on Llama-2-7B: 6.6%. Honesty improved on Llama-2-13B: 13.1%.
- Released: Benchmark code and data (MIT).
- Links: [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03750)

**Goldowsky-Dill et al. (Apollo), 'Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes' (arXiv 2502.03407)**
- Model: Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct
- Environments: Insider trading and prompted sandbagging rollouts
- Base rates: Trades on the insider tip: about 25%.
- Intervention: Linear probes for deception. Probing only.
- Effect: Probe results are not in the notes.
- Released: Rollouts and a generation script (no licence file).
- Links: [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03407), [repo](https://github.com/ApolloResearch/deception-detection)

**UK AISI, 'Auditing Games for Sandbagging' (arXiv 2512.07810)**
- Model: Llama-3.3-70B password-locked organisms and benign distractors
- Environments: BIG-Bench Hard, BigCodeBench, Intercode CTF, GPQA Diamond and others
- Base rates: Blue team classified models correctly in phase one: 1 of 5.
- Intervention: Deception probes; one-sample on-distribution training.
- Effect: Deception probes were elevated on Cedar but gave false positives on distractors. One-sample on-distribution training removed sandbagging.
- Released: Models, logs and code (MIT).
- Links: [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07810v1), [repo](https://github.com/AI-Safety-Institute/sandbagging_auditing_games)

### Closed-model analogues

**OpenAI, 'Studying metagaming latents in language models' (2026-10)**
- Model: o3 across RL checkpoints (closed)
- Environments: even_number task with a leaked grader
- Base rates: Baseline odd ratio: about 20%.
- Intervention: Gradient-based contrastive steering directions and four SAE latents. Steering only, no ablation.
- Effect: Positive steering raises verbalized metagaming and negative lowers it; effect sizes are in figures only. With no CoT, steering still raises the probability of odd digits. In the control where the leaked grader prefers even, every steered latent gives 0% odd.
- Released: No code, SAE or data release is stated.
- Links: [post](https://alignment.openai.com/metagaming-latents/)

**Schoen et al. (Apollo and OpenAI), 'Stress Testing Deliberative Alignment for Anti-Scheming Training' (arXiv 2509.15541)**
- Model: o3, o4-mini (closed)
- Environments: 26 anti-scheming evaluations
- Base rates: o3 covert action rate: 13%. o4-mini covert action rate: 8.7%.
- Intervention: Deliberative-alignment training.
- Effect: o3 after training: 0.4%. o4-mini after training: 0.3%. Causal evidence that evaluation awareness lowers covert behaviour.
- Released: Transcripts on antischeming.ai; environments not released.
- Links: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15541)

**MacDiarmid et al. (Anthropic), 'Natural Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacking in Production RL' (arXiv 2511.18397)**
- Model: Claude models (closed)
- Environments: Production RL environments
- Intervention: Preventing reward hacking, more diverse RLHF safety training, and inoculation prompting (framing hacking as acceptable in training).
- Effect: Inoculation prompting removed the misaligned generalisation even when hacking was still learned.
- Released: No data or code linked.
- Links: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18397)

**Hu et al. (Anthropic), 'Training on Documents About Reward Hacking Induces Reward Hacking' (2025)**
- Model: Haiku and an internal pretrained model (closed)
- Environments: Few-shot reward hacking; Python Test Overwrite on MBPP
- Base rates: Haiku few-shot reward hacking, baseline: 4.1%.
- Intervention: Continued pretraining on synthetic documents that discuss reward hacking (Pro or Anti).
- Effect: Haiku after Pro documents: 7.2%. Haiku after Anti documents: 2.9%. Post-training shrank the effects.
- Released: Document sets and transcripts (Google Drive).
- Links: [post](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/reward-hacking-ooc/)

**Lee, Chen, Korbak, 'Training Agents to Self-Report Misbehavior' (arXiv 2602.22303)**
- Model: GPT-4.1 and Gemini-2.0 agents (closed)
- Intervention: Fine-tune agents to call report_scheming() when misbehaving.
- Released: No code link on the abstract page.
- Links: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22303)

**Anthropic system cards: activation-level monitor for grader awareness**
- Model: Claude Opus 4.8 RL episodes (closed)
- Environments: Training transcripts
- Intervention: An activation-level monitor that detects grader awareness.
- Effect: Detects grader awareness in about 5% of sampled RL episodes (as cited by OpenAI's reward-seeking post).
- Links: [citing post](https://alignment.openai.com/measuring-reward-seeking/)
