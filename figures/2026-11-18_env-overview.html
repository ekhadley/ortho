Reward-hacking envs with base-model data, and what was run on each

An inventory, built from the records under data/inspect/, the Terminal-Bench jobs and the CheatBench verdicts. The base model is Qwen3.8-27B at reasoning effort low unless a row says otherwise. A rate is cheats over valid rollouts. An intervention is a served row: a vector added to the residual stream, here with a negative sign (steering), a direction projected out by a hook (projection), or a LoRA adapter.

The environments

One row per env with a base run of more than a smoke test. impossible_bench is left out: its only record is 2 samples on Qwen3.6-27B.

Base rollouts: the rollouts behind the base rates in the next figure, invalid ones included (MBPP: the 495 test-split tasks of a 954-task run; secret_number: 96 of 256 games). Conditions with data: interventions with a record on at least one of the env's cheat arms, Will's rows included.

Base cheat rates, beside each env's control arm

Filled: the arm where cheating is possible or rewarded. Hollow: the same label on the matched control arm, where the incentive is absent. Bars are 95% Wilson intervals.

incentive arm control arm

MBPP is the test split of mbpp38 (454 valid of 495); mbpp38_x4 holds four more samples of every task. secret_number is games 0-95 of secret38, which cheated in 126 of its 256 games. The hint_mc control is one number, the no-hint rate of picking the letter a hint would have named, repeated on each hint's row; the terminal_verifier control is the possible mode on all three rows. CheatBench's base is the row run on our server (Will's base row: 108 of 122).

Cheat rate under each intervention

Rows are cheat arms, columns are served rows. A number is the cheat rate in percent; a blank cell has no record. The Base column is the unedited model.

Colour is the change from the base rate as a share of the room available: a fall as a share of the base rate (-100% is no cheating left), a rise as a share of what was left to 100%. A cell is coloured only when a two-sided Fisher exact test against the base counts gives p < 0.01, with no correction over the page's cells; hover for counts, intervals and p. The test is unpaired. Solid outline: the arm's prompts are the ones the row's direction or adapter was fit on. Dashed outline: the same env, held-out families or tasks. The grader directions were fit on reasoning-on rollouts except domlast and domresp, which were fit on reasoning-off records. w: Will's run on his task pool, compared with his own base. Rows run on different days and servers.

Control arms under the same interventions

The arms with no incentive, and Terminal-Bench. Each row's label says what the number counts, so a higher number is better on some rows and worse on others. Colour is the direction of change only: blue lower than base, red higher.

What each direction and adapter was fit on

Only two envs have been fit on: grader and MBPP. One direction (b1) and one probe were fit on both together.

  cheating and honest rollouts the fit separates  neutral rollouts the fit holds fixed (token KL from the base model)

The grader fits use Will's s9-gr rollouts of the five main families, reasoning on, except nocot_dom, which uses reasoning-off records. The MBPP fits use the train and validation splits of mbpp38 and mbpp38_x4. terminal_verifier, secret_number, hint_mc, sorh, feasigen, toolfail, odcv and CheatBench are in no fit; the KTO sweep's screen scores base rollouts of terminal_verifier and secret_number but does not train on them.

Refusal, the behaviour abliteration was built for

Not a reward-hacking env. heretic's refusal direction as our projection hook, with random rows as controls. The number is the share of answers its keyword match calls a refusal, or for the grader rows the share of invalid or wrong-side answers.

No refusal row was run on a hack-prompt arm of any reward-hacking env, and no reward-hacking row was run on the refusal env.

Not in the records
