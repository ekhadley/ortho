In the regime our projection hook operates in, every position of the sequence nudged along the direction, the directions found on grader rollouts (trained KTO and DPO vectors, the projection row, the reasoning-off difference of means, Will's CoT-mean row) move the model's final state 1.2 to 2.1 times as much as random directions do and change its next-token distribution 2 to 5 times as much, far outside a random band only a few percent wide. That sensitivity is identical in cheating and clean rollouts, so it marks these as directions the network responds to, not as cheating-specific features. It is also a cross-position effect: nudging a single token brings the same directions down to 1.0 to 1.3, the level the pile-averaged J-lens reported, which is why that instrument saw nothing. The MBPP difference of means, the single-token probe, the model's own MLP neuron directions and the lens's high-gain directions are all at or near random.

What was measured, and why

A transformer carries one vector per token through its layers, the residual stream; each layer reads it and adds to it. Our interventions find a direction in that 5120-dimensional space tied to reward hacking (by averaging cheating minus honest activations, or by training a small vector) and remove the model's component along it while it generates. The question here is whether those directions are ones the network actually responds to, or merely correlate with cheating while the later layers ignore them.

The operationalisation is a finite difference, the same thing a Jacobian computes but on the real model in a real context: nudge the residual at layer L along the direction, rerun the layers above, and compare the final residual to the clean run. A direction the network amplifies gives a large shift per unit of nudge. Earlier, a pile-averaged Jacobian lens (the J-lens for Qwen3.6) scored every one of our directions, and the model's own neurons, at random; the diagram shows the in-context version run on Qwen3.8 itself.

With every position nudged, the grader directions are clearly more sensitive than random

The left panel is the finding. Four grader directions found by different recipes sit at 1.2 to 2.1 times the random median at every layer, and the random band they are compared against is 0.94 to 1.11 wide at layer 32 and 0.99 to 1.02 at layer 60. The ratios do not depend on the nudge size (2% to 20% of the residual norm give the same numbers) or on its sign, and the next-token KL tells the same story at 2 to 5 times random over the reasoning tokens.

The right panel holds the nulls. mbpp_dom, the paired difference of means that works as a projection hook on MBPP, is at 1.0 to 1.1 everywhere, including the token it was fit at. mbpp_kto is 1.2 to 1.3 in gain (though 3 to 4.5 times random in KL at layers 32 to 48). The model's own MLP decoder and encoder directions are at random in context, as they were under the lens. The mean residuals (the hook's pins) are at random until layer 48 and 1.4 to 1.6 at layers 52 to 56, exactly what the lens predicted. The lens's high-gain directions (pullbacks) are 1.1 to 1.4 whether they come from token clusters or from random vectors, so that is the construction, not features.

The effect lives across positions, which is why a same-position Jacobian missed it

Nudging only the newline after <think> (the token whose next token is the model's first reasoning token) and reading the gain at that token, the same directions fall to 1.05 to 1.14, and the pullbacks to 1.0. A same-position map, which is what the J-lens is, sees roughly this. The extra factor with every position nudged comes from attention carrying a coherent shift from many tokens into each later one; random directions get the same mechanical treatment and gain less from it.

At that single token the next-token KL does separate some directions: nocot_dom_last (fit at the last prompt token with reasoning off) and grader_kto beat the random 95th percentile in 80 to 95% of rollouts at layers 48 to 60, though the absolute KL there is about 1e-4 nats, a very small change to the first reasoning token.

Not more sensitive when the model is cheating

The hypothesis that motivated the test would predict a cheat-specific direction to be one the network responds to more in cheating contexts. It is not: within either environment the cheating and clean cells agree within 0.11. The grader directions are somewhat higher in grader rollouts than in MBPP ones and mbpp_kto the reverse, a mild environment effect.

Per token, without averaging

Because the gain at a token depends on what that token is (it rises along the reasoning and at the newlines, see the trace), the per-token test asks at each reasoning token whether the direction beats the random 95th percentile at that same token. With every position nudged, the grader directions do so at 60 to 100% of tokens; the saved random direction and the MLP neurons at the 5% null rate; mbpp_dom at 7 to 37%. With one position nudged, most directions drop toward the null, the pins excepted at layers 52 to 60.

What this does and does not say

  • Sensitivity is real but generic. The grader directions are directions the network amplifies in our contexts, but equally when the model is honest, so passing this test does not certify a cheating feature. It does separate the trained and averaged grader directions from random, which the lens could not.
  • Hook efficacy and sensitivity are different questions. mbpp_dom cuts MBPP cheating from 27% to 3% as a hook while reading at random here; grader_kto reads highest here and also works. Neither implies the other.
  • The averaged J-lens is the wrong instrument for this. Its same-position, context-averaged gain matches the single-position in-context numbers (near 1) and cannot see the cross-position effect the hook regime produces.
  • Nulls: the single-token probe (1.02), MLP neurons (0.97 to 1.06), Will's G9 (1.0 to 1.2, weak), lens pullbacks (construction), sign asymmetry (none), nudge size (none from 2% to 20%).
